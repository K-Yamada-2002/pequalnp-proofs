# A seven-dimensional level space needs at least fifteen varying entries

Let $F(X)$ be the sum over injections of the four-row products in $J+X$, minus 120, where $J$ is the all-ones $4\times5$ matrix. Suppose $F$ vanishes on a seven-dimensional complex linear space $L$. A position belongs to its support if its entry is nonzero on some member of $L$.

The [support enumeration](support_bound.py) excludes every support of size at most 13. Of the 53 orbits on 14 positions under independent row and column permutations, only four admit an isotropic seven-plane for the Hessian at zero on the total-sum-zero hyperplane. In each of the four cases its one-dimensional radical must lie in $L$. In two cases the permanent varies on that line. We exclude the remaining two orbits below. At size 15, the [first five exclusions](fifteen.md) and [nine full-row exclusions](row_normal15.md) together rule out 14 of the 27 support orbits. The existence of a seven-dimensional space remains open.

## Row masks (1,15,15,31)

Masks use the least significant bit for column zero. Eliminate the last of the 14 supported entries using the linear term, which forces the total sum to vanish. Let $r$ span the radical at zero. A derivative at a point of $L$ imposes another linear equation on $L$, and the Hessian there vanishes on $L\times L$. Intersect the gradient kernels at $0,r,2r$. The resulting space $V$ has dimension 12. The Hessian at zero restricted to $V$ has rank 10. Its two-dimensional radical $K$ lies in $L$, since the nondegenerate quotient admits isotropic spaces of dimension at most five.

In the echelon basis $k_1,k_2$ of $K$ specified in [support14.py](support14.py), take the Hessians at zero and at $k_1+2k_2$. Both descend to $V/K$. Write them as $B,C$, respectively. The image of $L$ is maximal isotropic for $B$ and equals its orthogonal complement. Since $C$ also vanishes on it, $B^{-1}C$ preserves it. The generalized eigenspaces split orthogonally for $B$, and their projectors are polynomials in the operator. A half-dimensional isotropic space invariant under the operator must attain half the dimension in each primary summand, since none can contribute more. The eigenvalue $1/3$ has algebraic multiplicity two and eigenspace dimension one. Its primary summand must contribute an invariant line, necessarily that eigenspace. Lifting it gives a third forced direction, together with $K$ spanning $K'$.

Intersect gradient kernels again, this time using zero and all sums of up to three echelon basis elements of $K'$ with repetition. The new ambient dimension is 11. The Hessian at zero has rank eight and radical $K'$. On the eight-dimensional quotient, form the pencil using the Hessian at zero and the weighted sum of Hessians at zero and the three echelon basis vectors, with weights 1,2,3,4. Its four eigenvalues are $8,9,10,35/3$, each with a nondegenerate two-dimensional eigenspace. The image of $L$ is a sum of four isotropic lines, one in each eigenspace. Each binary quadratic form has exactly two such lines over the complex numbers. The script computes both in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$, including a line at infinity where appropriate. For each of the resulting 16 seven-planes, direct evaluation on the combination of its seven basis vectors with weights 1 through 7 gives a nonzero value of $F$.

## Row masks (7,7,7,31)

Let $A$ be the upper left three-by-three block, and $t$ the sum of the bottom row's last two entries. Only their sum affects $F$, since the first three rows are unchanged in those columns. Quotient out their difference direction. The total-sum-zero hyperplane now has dimension 12 and its Hessian is nondegenerate. A seven-plane would have to contain the difference direction, and project to a maximal isotropic six-plane $U$.

The quartic term factors as $t\operatorname{per}(A)$, by assigning the first three rows to the first three columns. As polynomials on $U$, one of these factors must vanish identically. If $t=0$ on $U$, the entire problem reduces to the twelve positions in the first three columns. Their total-sum-zero hyperplane has dimension 11 and Hessian rank 11. An isotropic six-plane is impossible there. Hence $\operatorname{per}(A)=0$ identically on $U$.

Let $R$ be the three-dimensional isotropic space of bottom-row variations in the quotient, with $A=0$ and total sum zero. Pairing with these variations shows that $R^\perp$ is defined by all three column sums of $A$ being zero. Projection onto $A$ identifies $R^\perp/R$ with the six-dimensional space of column-centered three-by-three matrices. The image of $U\cap R^\perp$ is an isotropic **three-plane** there. Indeed, if $h=\dim(U\cap R)$, the pairing between $R$ and $U$ has rank $3-h$, since $U=U^\perp$. Its kernel in $U$ has dimension $3+h$, and quotienting by $U\cap R$ gives three. The cubic $\operatorname{per}(A)$ vanishes on this three-plane.

For a primitive cube root of unity $\omega$, write $A_{ij}=z_j\omega^i+u_j\omega^{-i}$ for $i,j=0,1,2$. These six coordinates parametrize all column-centered matrices. The cubic is $6(z_0z_1z_2+u_0u_1u_2)$. Up to nonzero scaling the quadratic form is $z^T D u$ where $D=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T$. This follows by subtracting the sum of squared row sums from the sum of squared entries. The bottom-row terms cancel in the quotient.

Restrict the six linear forms $z_j,u_j$ to the proposed three-plane. If either triple product vanishes identically then both do, so some $z_i$ and some $u_j$ vanish. On that coordinate four-space the pairing is a two-by-two minor of $D$, and every such minor is nonzero. Thus its maximal isotropic dimension is two. In the other case unique factorization in the polynomial ring on the three-plane pairs each $u_j$ with a nonzero scalar multiple of a $z_i$, counting multiplicities. Write $u=Mz$ with $M$ an invertible monomial matrix. Projection onto $z$ is injective on the three-plane and hence surjective onto $\mathbb C^3$. The quadratic identity forces $DM$ to be skew-symmetric. This is impossible because $DM$ is invertible and a skew-symmetric matrix of odd size has zero determinant.

## Reproduction

Run `sage -python support_bound.py` and `sage -python support14.py` from this directory. The ranks, spectral decompositions, candidate evaluations and polynomial identities use exact arithmetic. The dimension and unique-factorization arguments above are mathematical arguments over $\mathbb C$. No Lean acceptance is claimed.
